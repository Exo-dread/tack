Privacy

Tack keeps your tasks and notes on your own devices. There are no Tack accounts, no ads, no tracking and no analytics, and Tack has no server that receives or stores your data.

This page covers Tack for Windows and Tack Focus, its companion app at tackfocus.app.

On your devices

  • Tack Focus keeps your tasks, notes, restore points and settings in your browser’s storage on that device.
  • Tack for Windows keeps them in one file, %LOCALAPPDATA%\Tack\tack.json, with a backup copy beside it (or in a TackData folder next to Tack.exe in portable mode). It never connects to the internet.
  • Nothing leaves your device unless you turn on sync, or export a file yourself.
  • The website. tackfocus.app is a set of static files hosted by Cloudflare. Loading it is an ordinary web request, which Cloudflare handles as any web host does (see Cloudflare’s privacy policy). Tack sets no cookies and loads no analytics or third-party scripts. Once loaded, Tack Focus works offline.

Sync with OneDrive (optional)

Sync keeps Tack Focus and Tack for Windows up to date with each other through your own OneDrive. It stays off unless you turn it on (Settings › Sync, in each app), and both apps work fully without it.

  • Your data goes to your OneDrive, not to Tack. Each device saves your tasks and notes as a file in one folder of your OneDrive, Apps › Tack Focus, and reads the other devices’ files from there. Tack Focus talks to Microsoft directly; there’s no Tack server in between.
  • Tack for Windows doesn’t sign in. It reads and writes that same folder on your PC, which the OneDrive app keeps up to date. It still never connects to the internet itself.
  • What’s in the files. Your tasks and notes; each device’s name and Tack version (a PC’s name includes the computer’s name, such as “Tack for Windows on DESKTOP-1”); and a short record of each deleted task (an id and a time, never the text), so it can’t come back from a device that was offline.
  • Who can read them. They’re ordinary files in your OneDrive, so you can open them yourself. Microsoft stores them under its own privacy statement. Any app you’ve allowed into your whole OneDrive, and anyone who gets into your Microsoft account, could read them too. They aren’t end-to-end encrypted yet; optional encryption with a passphrase only you know is planned for a later version.
  • Sign-in tokens stay in the browser’s storage on the device that signed in, and are sent only to Microsoft.

Microsoft permissions

When you turn on sync in Tack Focus, you sign in on Microsoft’s own page (Tack never sees your password), and Microsoft asks you to allow these permissions. They’re the narrowest that make sync work:

  • Its own folder in your OneDrive (Files.ReadWrite.AppFolder): read and write files in Apps › Tack Focus, a folder OneDrive sets aside for Tack. Tack can’t see, open or change anything else in your OneDrive.
  • Stay signed in (offline_access): so sync keeps working without you signing in again every day.
  • Sign you in and see your basic profile (openid, profile): your name and the address you sign in with. Settings shows them so you know which account is syncing; they stay on the device.

Tack doesn’t ask for your mail, contacts, calendar, other files, or anything else in your Microsoft account.

Deleting your data

Tack holds no copy of your data, so there’s nothing to ask Tack to delete. It’s all on your devices and, with sync, in your OneDrive, where you can delete it yourself:

  • A task or note: delete it in the app. With sync on, the deletion reaches your other devices.
  • Everything in Tack Focus on a device: clear the site data for tackfocus.app in your browser’s settings (in Chrome on Android: Settings › Site settings › All sites › tackfocus.app). Removing the installed app alone may leave the data in the browser. Export first if you want to keep a copy.
  • Everything in Tack for Windows: close Tack and delete the folder %LOCALAPPDATA%\Tack (or the TackData folder in portable mode). Deleting Tack.exe alone leaves your data in place.
  • Sync data in OneDrive: turn off sync on every device first (each keeps its own tasks and notes, and Tack Focus forgets the sign-in). Then delete the Apps › Tack Focus folder in OneDrive and empty OneDrive’s recycle bin, which removes the files and their earlier versions for good. A device that’s still syncing would write its file again.
  • Tack’s access to your Microsoft account: remove it at account.live.com/consent/Manage (personal accounts) or myapps.microsoft.com (work or school accounts).
